# Quartz Assessment release notes

Major capabilities, newest first. The admin screen fetches this file from
/release-notes.md when it loads: every entry appears in **What's new**, and one a signed-in person hasn't seen yet puts the
announcement bar across the top of Admin until they read or dismiss it.

Each entry is a `##` title, a `Released: YYYY-MM-DD` line, and a short
explanation written for the people using the app. The title is the entry's
identity — renaming one announces it again to everyone who already read it.

## Share an idea

Released: 2026-09-17

The 💡 in the bottom-right corner of every admin page, and **Share an idea** in the sidebar. Anything the app should do and doesn't — a report that should say something it doesn't, a step that takes longer than it should.

It opens over whatever you're looking at, so you never lose your place, and it records which page you were on.

It asks two things: the idea in a few words, and what problem it solves. The second is the one that matters. An idea arrives as a solution, and a solution can only be weighed against the problem underneath it — which is often solvable a better way than the one either of us thought of first.

Filing one promises nothing. We read every one, we build the ones where the problem turns out to be shared, and anything we build shows up here in What's new.

## Clearer reports

Released: 2026-09-17

Each respondent's own report now carries the assessment's full name on screen, not only on the printed cover — so someone holding two links can tell which one they just answered. The instrument's name used to stand in for it, and it could not tell two runs apart.

Each question's commentary now sits in a panel of its own, labelled, so nobody has to work out which lines are their own answers and which are ours.

Reading is capped at two articles per activity. An activity that has collected eight offers the first two, in the order set in Settings → Resources, so which two is an editorial decision rather than an accident. A page of every article we have reads as a catalogue, and a catalogue gets read as advertising.

## One way to name an assessment

Released: 2026-09-17

Every assessment is now named the same way, in three parts: the instrument, the client, and which run this is — **Team Gap Analysis - Blackbaud - Initial assessment 260917**. **New assessment** collects the three separately, fills the instrument in for you, and shows the finished name before you create it.

The client is no longer optional; use **Public** when there is no single client, such as an open workshop or a talk. A one-line description is optional and appears under the title on reports. That name is the heading on every report a buyer, a team leader, and each respondent reads, so two runs for the same client can finally be told apart a year later.

Assessments also start **active** now. There was a draft state before, and it never held anything back — the access code worked from the moment the assessment existed — so the only status that changes anything is **closed**.

## Discussion for instruments

Released: 2026-09-16

The Chaos Assessment, Portfolio Health Check, Product Success Quiz, and Idea Reality Check now have a **Discussion** tab, like the team gap analysis. Questions come in the report's order, most split first, and each one takes a flag, a status, notes, a decision, and who owns it. Close the assessment and the report adds the team's decisions and open issues after the questions.

## Admin on your phone

Released: 2026-09-16

Admin now fits a phone. The sidebar folds into a menu behind the ☰ button, and the Assessments page shows each assessment as a card with its client, type, status, responses, and last activity. Tap a card to open it. Assessments with new responses open on Results.

## The Assessments page

Released: 2026-09-16

Admin now opens on one table of every assessment you can see, with its client, instrument, responses so far, last activity, and status. Search it, switch between **Mine** and **Everyone's**, and narrow it to one client with the client dropdown.

A red number means new responses: people who finished since you last opened that assessment's Results. It shows on the row, in the sidebar, and in the browser tab's title. Pin the assessments you're running to keep them in the sidebar, and press ⌘K (Ctrl+K on Windows) from anywhere in Admin to jump to any assessment or start a new one.

## Six instruments

Released: 2026-09-09

**New assessment** now lets you choose what you're running. Alongside the team gap analysis and the personal assessment are four instruments that ask their own fixed questions: the **Chaos Assessment**, the **Portfolio Health Check**, the **Product Success Quiz**, and the **Idea Reality Check**.

Their report is an agenda rather than a verdict. Each question gets one bar showing where the room landed, ordered by how much the team disagreed with itself, so the most split questions, which are what the session is for, come first. Each respondent can review and change their answers, and gets their own copy.

## Save and finish later

Released: 2026-09-03

Respondents no longer have to finish in one sitting. The survey gives them a link to come back to, and it picks up on the first page they haven't finished. The intro also quotes a realistic time for the activity set you chose, so nobody is promised twenty minutes for a forty-five-minute survey.

## Who owns each activity today

Released: 2026-08-20

The team gap analysis now asks who owns each activity today, rather than who should. Every activity already carries a recommended owner, so the report can set what's recommended against what actually happens: win/loss analysis recommended to product marketing but done by sales is a finding. Respondents pick from job titles, and can also answer **I don't know** or **No one**, both of which are findings in their own right.

## Every respondent gets their own summary

Released: 2026-08-14

When someone finishes a team gap analysis or a personal assessment, they see a summary of their own answers, laid out to read comfortably on a phone and ready to save as a PDF. They can come back and change an answer they've already given. People arrive at the debrief already knowing where they said the gaps are.

## Resources and Try this on the personal report

Released: 2026-08-11

The personal report is now advisory. It sorts each person's activities into five categories, from strengths to development opportunities, and pairs each opportunity with suggested reading and a one-line **Try this**: one concrete step to start with.

## The LEARN facet

Released: 2026-08-09

Alongside the six Quartz facets, the library now includes **LEARN**: the activities that run across the whole cycle, like reviewing whether the last release did what you expected. It reports as its own section, and each activity preset card now says what the set is for.

## Personal assessment

Released: 2026-08-07

A second kind of assessment over the same activity library. Instead of rating the team, each person rates their own **experience**, **skills**, and **interest** in each activity. The output is a development profile that belongs to that person, and it is attributed rather than anonymous. A personal assessment can be linked to a team gap analysis, so you can cross what a team needs against what its people can do.

## Tags

Released: 2026-08-07

Group related assessments with tags: a cohort, a program, or a support group. An assessment can carry several, the search box matches tag names, and **Settings → Tags** shows how many assessments use each one and removes the ones nothing uses.

## Save as PDF

Released: 2026-08-05

Both the respondent's answers and the Report and Action Plan now have a **Save as PDF** button. The saved file is named after the assessment, with a proper cover, page headers and footers, and nothing from the screen that doesn't belong on paper.

## Team Leader Dashboard

Released: 2026-07-30

Share one link with the client's team leader. From it they can see the assessment's activities, flag the ones they want to discuss, and add their team, each of whom gets their own survey link. The team leader never sees anyone's individual answers.

## Organizations and facilitators

Released: 2026-07-15

Quartz Assessment now supports many consulting organizations, each with its own facilitators. A facilitator sees only the assessments they created or were invited to, and an organization admin sees their whole organization's work and manages its facilitators. Add a colleague as a **Collaborator** on the Overview tab to run an engagement together.

## Facilitator Guide

Released: 2026-06-15

A step-by-step guide to running an engagement, from setup and fielding through the debrief and what to tell participants. It's linked from the Admin sidebar and kept up to date as the app changes.

## Discussion

Released: 2026-06-11

The facilitated debrief has its own workspace. Flag the activities you plan to focus on, record the conversation in **Discussion Notes** as it happens, and capture what the team agreed in **Decision / Action**. Decisions flow into the client's report as a "What we decided" section once you close the assessment.

## Results and the Report and Action Plan

Released: 2026-06-11

The Results tab shows who has responded, each activity's importance, execution, and gap, and where the team disagrees about ownership. The Report and Action Plan turns the same data into a report for the client's leadership, with the key finding, what it means, and the activities that need attention, and it never identifies an individual.

## Team gap analysis

Released: 2026-06-11

The original Quartz Assessment. A product team rates each activity on how important it is and how well it's done today, and names who owns it. The distance between importance and execution is the finding, and it drives the engagement.
